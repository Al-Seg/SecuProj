Detecting Network Scanning Activity

Using Nmap and Wireshark to study TCP scan patterns and explore ways to distinguish suspicious scanning from normal network traffic.

01 / OVERVIEW

Why this matters

Port scanning is often a legitimate administrative task, but it can also be part of an attacker's reconnaissance. Looking at packet behavior can help identify patterns worth investigating.

01

Research question

Which traffic patterns can help distinguish Nmap TCP scans from ordinary connections, and how reliably can simple rules detect them?

02

What will be compared

Normal network activity, TCP connect scans, and SYN scans in a controlled test environment.

03

How it will be evaluated

Compare packet signatures, detection rates, and false positives to understand the usefulness and limits of the indicators.

02 / EXPERIMENT PLAN

From traffic to evidence

The work is organized into four stages. These describe the planned experiments, not completed results.

  1. 01

    Build a controlled lab

    Configure an isolated virtual network with an authorized scan source and target.

    SETUP
  2. 02

    Capture a baseline

    Record ordinary TCP activity in Wireshark to establish comparison traffic.

    BASELINE
  3. 03

    Run controlled scans

    Use Nmap TCP connect and SYN scans, and observe ports, TCP flags, responses, and timing.

    TESTING
  4. 04

    Compare detection rules

    Test whether indicators separate scans from baseline traffic, and document false positives.

    ANALYSIS
